This is the Add post box. Do the four numbered spots in the picture, in order:

  1. Paste the post. Tap inside the big Description box and paste the words you copied in Step 1.
  2. Add the photo. Tap “Select images and videos” and choose the picture named on the post card.

Why this actually works

Two minutes on why these little posts matter more than they look.

Why does keeping our Google profile active even matter?
When someone searches “tax advisor near me” or “tax help in Coral Gables,” Google picks just a few businesses to show at the top, on the map. It favors profiles that look alive and real — ones that post regularly, add fresh photos, answer questions, and collect reviews. Every post is a small signal to Google that this is a real, open, engaged business, so it shows us to more people. A profile that goes quiet slowly slides down and gets shown less.
Why two posts a week — isn’t once enough?
Google rewards steady freshness, not bursts. Two posts a week keeps a constant “recently active” signal and gives the profile more content for Google to match against searches. The rhythm is the point: every week, forever, beats ten posts in one day and then silence. It also compounds — the longer we stay consistent, the more Google trusts the profile and the higher it ranks.
Why do the posts sound like a problem or a question, not a sales ad?
People scroll right past ads — but they stop when something names their own situation. A line like “your business grew, did your financial team keep up?” makes the right person feel seen, and it quietly filters for exactly the kind of owner we want. It also builds trust: we’re teaching, not pitching, which is how a high-trust service like tax strategy earns clients. And plain words (no jargon) mean anyone understands it in two seconds.
Why does every post need a photo?
Posts with a photo get far more views than text alone, and photo activity is one of the strongest signals to Google that a profile is real and active. A photo is also what makes someone stop scrolling and actually read. A text-only post barely registers — with a picture, it works.
Why real photos instead of nice stock images?
Google can tell the difference, and so can people. Real photos of the team, the office, and the work build trust and tie the profile to our actual location — both of which help us rank locally. Stock photos signal nothing: they don’t say “real, local, active business,” and to a sharp business owner they can feel generic and forgettable.
Why a button and a link on every single post?
A post that only informs is a dead end. The button — Learn more, Book, or Call now — turns a reader into a lead. It’s the one action that connects Google to the rest of our funnel (the free checkup or the booking calendar). It’s also measurable: we can see which posts drive clicks and calls, and do more of what works.
What do the Q&A entries actually do?
Google lets the business post its own questions and answers. That does two useful things: it answers common objections before someone has to ask, and it adds helpful, keyword-rich text that Google can match to more searches. It’s a quiet way to look responsive and show up for more terms.
How does this connect to the ads and the bigger plan?
Organic presence makes paid ads work better. When someone clicks our Google ad and then sees the profile is active — real photos, real reviews, recent posts — they trust it, and a cold click converts warmer. GMB is the free, compounding foundation underneath the paid campaigns. It also feeds the same message: the ad and the post name the same problem, so everything reinforces everything.
When will we see results — is this instant?
Honestly, no — and it’s important to expect that. GMB is a slow, compounding play: rankings and trust build over weeks and months of consistency, not overnight. The upside is that it’s durable — once it’s working it keeps working without ad spend. The goal here is steady and forever, not a quick spike. Every post is a brick; the wall takes time.
