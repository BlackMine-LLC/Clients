— LA HISTORIA

She didn’t wait
for permission.

Mariah Angelique Pérez was born in Miami to a Cuban father and a Puerto Rican mother, and named after the only voice her mom played louder than the radio — Mariah Carey. Spanish first, English second, el perreo always.

At nine, she dreamed she was on a stage in front of “mad people.” At sixteen, she believed it enough to leave home and chase it. Her mother was scared to let her go. She went anyway — “if I didn’t do it, no one would.”

A producer — Nely, “El Arma Secreta” — showed her she could sing fire en español. She fell in love with the language, signed to Universal, and dropped “Perreito” — Gold in the U.S. and Puerto Rico, charting in 14 countries. Then “El Makinon” with Karol G — two women, a modern Thelma & Louise — became one of the biggest reggaetón records of its era, nearing a billion views.

She never counted the men in the room. She called her shot at nineteen. It landed.

“I want my voice to be timeless. For people to hear me and just know — that’s Mariah.”
